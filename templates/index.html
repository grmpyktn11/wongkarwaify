How it works

A computational photography project · every number is measured, not chosen

We take one frame from each film every 60 seconds. That is 99 frames per film. Averaging four numbers across them gives us the grade.

  1. White balance

    R ← R × 1.4046    B ← B × 0.5462

    Average a whole film and you get its colour cast. We multiply your red and blue channels by the film's numbers.

    This adds the film's cast on top of yours. It assumes your photo starts neutral.

  2. Saturation

    S ← S × (Sfilm ÷ Sphoto)

    We measure how saturated your photo is, then scale it to match the film. This stage reads your image first, so a vivid photo gets toned down.

  3. Colour grade

    shadows → green  ·  midtones → magenta  ·  highlights → yellow

    We split the image into dark, mid and bright areas, then tint each one differently. Cool shadows with warm highlights is the oldest trick in grading.

    Both films use the same tints right now.

  4. Contrast curve

    f(x) = xp ÷ (xp + (1−x)p)

    An S-curve, one per channel. Red is 1.2, green is 1.0 and does not change, blue is 1.4. Blue bends most, so blue shadows go darker and blue highlights go brighter.

  5. Brightness

    γ = log(0.5) ÷ log(L̄)    out = in1/γ

    Both films sit at 15.9% brightness. They are shot at night, in corridors, through doorways. That gives an exponent of 2.65, and it does most of the work.

    Also the harshest step. It turns 0.50 into 0.159, so a daylight photo goes to mud.

Then mix it back

We blend the result with your original, 70 to 30. That brings the brightness back without losing the colour.
